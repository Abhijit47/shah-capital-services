# Corporate Deposits

Corporate Deposits are fixed income investment instruments where funds are deposited with companies for a fixed tenure at a predetermined rate of interest, subject to the issuer’s terms and conditions.

The interest rate, tenure, payout frequency, and investment structure may vary depending on:

- Issuer profile
- Credit rating
- Deposit tenure
- Investor category
- Investment amount

Corporate deposits are commonly considered by investors looking for relatively stable fixed-income opportunities with potentially higher yields compared to traditional savings products.

---

# Benefits Of Investing In Company Fixed Deposits

- Competitive interest rates
- Short and medium-term deposit options
- Flexible tenure structures
- Predictable income generation
- Diversification within fixed income allocation
- Low minimum investment options in select products
- Interest payout flexibility in certain schemes

In certain cases:

- Lock-in period may be as low as 6 months
- No TDS may apply if interest income remains within applicable limits
- Investments may be spread across issuers for tax efficiency and diversification

# Fixed Maturity Plans (FMPs)

Fixed Maturity Plans (FMPs) are closed-ended debt mutual fund schemes with a fixed maturity period. These schemes primarily invest in debt and money market instruments maturing on or before the maturity date of the scheme.

FMPs are often considered by investors seeking relatively stable, tax-efficient fixed income opportunities over a defined investment horizon.

---

# Why FMPs?

FMPs are often viewed as an alternative to traditional fixed deposits, with the key distinction being that returns are indicative and not guaranteed.

Since the underlying investments are generally held until maturity, fund houses are able to provide indicative return expectations based on prevailing yields at the time of investment.

FMPs may offer:

- Better post-tax efficiency in certain situations
- Defined investment tenure
- Relatively lower interest rate volatility
- Structured debt allocation
- Diversification across fixed income instruments

Though returns are not guaranteed, FMPs are generally considered relatively lower-risk fixed income products depending on portfolio quality and market conditions.

---

# Where Do FMPs Invest?

FMPs typically invest in:

- Certificates of Deposit (CDs)
- Commercial Papers (CPs)
- Money Market Instruments
- Corporate Bonds
- Bank Fixed Deposits
- Debt Securities aligned to scheme maturity

Depending on the tenure of the FMP, the fund manager invests in securities with matching or similar maturity profiles.

---

# Tenure Of FMPs

FMPs may have varying maturities ranging from:

- One month
- Short-term durations
- Multi-year tenures up to three years or more

They are closed-ended schemes, meaning investments are accepted only during the New Fund Offer (NFO) period.

FMP NFOs are often positioned for:

- Corporates
- High net-worth investors
- Investors seeking tax-efficient fixed income allocation

However, minimum investment amounts are often accessible for retail investors as well.

# 54EC Bonds

54EC Bonds are capital gain tax-saving bonds that allow eligible investors to claim exemption on long-term capital gains under Section 54EC of the Income Tax Act, subject to prevailing regulations and conditions.

These bonds are generally issued by government-backed entities and are commonly used by investors looking to save tax arising from the sale of long-term capital assets.

---

# Key Features Of 54EC Bonds

- Eligible for capital gains tax exemption under Section 54EC
- Fixed interest payout structure
- Relatively high credit quality issuers
- Available in demat and physical forms (subject to issuer)
- Fixed lock-in / maturity period
- Suitable for capital gain planning

# Important Conditions Under Section 54EC

- Applicable on eligible long-term capital gains
- Investment must generally be made within 6 months from transfer of asset
- Maximum eligible exemption subject to prevailing limits
- Bonds carry a lock-in period
- Premature transfer or pledge may impact tax exemption eligibility

Tax laws and limits are subject to change based on prevailing regulations.

# Debentures

Debentures are debt instruments issued by corporations or institutions to raise capital. Unlike secured borrowings backed by physical collateral, debentures are generally backed by the creditworthiness and reputation of the issuer.

Debentures form part of the issuer’s debt capital structure and typically provide fixed interest payments over a specified tenure.

These instruments are commonly considered by investors seeking:

- Fixed income opportunities
- Predictable interest payouts
- Diversification within debt allocation
- Medium to long-term investment options

---

# Types Of Debentures

## Convertible Debentures

Convertible debentures are debt instruments that can be converted into equity shares of the issuing company after a predetermined period and subject to specified conditions.

These may offer:

- Potential equity participation
- Lower coupon rates compared to non-convertible debentures
- Hybrid investment characteristics

---

## Non-Convertible Debentures (NCDs)

Non-convertible debentures cannot be converted into equity shares and remain pure fixed income instruments throughout their tenure.

These typically offer:

- Fixed interest payouts
- Defined maturity structure
- Higher coupon rates relative to convertible debentures in certain cases
